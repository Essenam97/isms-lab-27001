# 4.1 Understanding the organization and its context

Clause 4.1 asks the organization to determine the external and internal issues that are relevant to its purpose and that affect its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS. Since Amendment 1 (2024), it must also determine whether climate change is a relevant issue.

Each issue below has an ID so the risk register (clause 6.1) can reference it.

## External issues

| ID | Issue | Effect on the ISMS |
|---|---|---|
| EXT-01 | Law No. 2019-014 on personal data protection applies to all employee data stored in Lagune Pay. The supervisory authority, IPDCP, started operating in March 2025. | Processing must be declared to the IPDCP; data subject rights must be handled; security of processing is a legal duty. |
| EXT-02 | Law 2019-014 allows transfers of personal data abroad only to countries with an adequate level of protection. Production is hosted in Europe. | The legal basis for the transfer must be documented; supplier contract must cover it (controls 5.19 to 5.23, 5.34). |
| EXT-03 | Law No. 2018-026 of 7 December 2018 on cybersecurity and the fight against cybercrime. | Defines offences such as unauthorized access; relevant for incident handling and evidence collection (5.26 to 5.28). |
| EXT-04 | Large customers require security evidence in tenders. | ISMS scope and SoA must cover what customers audit: the product and its operation. |
| EXT-05 | Phishing and mobile money fraud targeting payroll staff of customer companies. | Attackers may try to change bank details in Lagune Pay to divert salaries. |
| EXT-06 | Single fibre provider for the office; power cuts in Lomé. | Availability of internal tools and support team (7.11, 8.14). |
| EXT-07 | Dependence on one cloud provider for production. | Supplier failure or price change affects all customers (5.23, 5.30). |
| EXT-08 | Climate change: heavier rainy seasons and flooding in parts of Lomé. | Assessed as relevant for the office (power, access, equipment) and not relevant for production, which is hosted abroad. Reviewed yearly. |

## Internal issues

| ID | Issue | Effect on the ISMS |
|---|---|---|
| INT-01 | No dedicated security staff; ISMS manager at 40% time. | Limits how many controls can be run manually; favours automation. |
| INT-02 | Developers have had direct access to the production database for debugging. | Major access control gap (8.2, 8.3, 8.31). |
| INT-03 | Support staff can view all customer data to answer tickets. | Need-to-know not applied (5.15, 8.3). |
| INT-04 | Fast release cycle: about 3 releases per week. | Change management must not block releases (8.32). |
| INT-05 | Personal phones used for MFA and email. | BYOD rules needed (6.7, 8.1). |
| INT-06 | Management supports the project because of customer demand. | Budget approved for 12 months; to be confirmed in the ISMS policy (clause 5). |
| INT-07 | Backups exist but restore has never been tested. | Control 8.13 not effective until tested. |
