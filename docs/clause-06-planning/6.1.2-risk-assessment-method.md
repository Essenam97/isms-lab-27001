# 6.1.2 Information security risk assessment method

Clause 6.1.2 asks the organization to define and apply a risk assessment process that sets risk acceptance criteria, produces consistent and comparable results, identifies risks to confidentiality, integrity and availability within the ISMS scope, names a risk owner for each risk, and analyses and evaluates the risks. This document is that process for Lagune Payroll SARL. ISO/IEC 27005:2022 was used as guidance.

## 1. Approach

Lagune Payroll uses an event-based approach: each risk is written as a scenario (a threat source, what it does, and the consequence for the business). Assets are listed in each risk entry but there is no separate asset-by-asset assessment. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 does not require an asset-based method, and with one part-time ISMS manager a scenario list is easier to keep current.

Inputs to risk identification:

- external and internal issues in [4.1](../clause-04-context/4.1-internal-external-issues.md) (IDs EXT-xx and INT-xx);
- requirements of interested parties in [4.2](../clause-04-context/4.2-interested-parties.md) (IDs IP-xx);
- incidents and near misses recorded in the incident log;
- findings from internal audits and supplier assessments.

## 2. Likelihood scale

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Less than once in 10 years |
| 2 | Unlikely | Once in 5 to 10 years |
| 3 | Possible | Once in 1 to 5 years |
| 4 | Likely | About once a year |
| 5 | Almost certain | Several times a year |

Likelihood is rated taking the controls already in place into account.

## 3. Impact scale

The impact level is the highest level reached in any column. Amounts are in XOF; annual revenue is 410 million XOF.

| Level | Financial | Customers and service | Personal data | Legal and contractual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Negligible | Under 0.5 M | No customer notices | No personal data involved | None |
| 2 Minor | 0.5 to 4 M | Internal tools unavailable under 1 day; one customer complains | Data of under 10 data subjects exposed internally | Minor contract breach, no penalty |
| 3 Moderate | 4 to 15 M | Lagune Pay unavailable 4 to 24 hours outside the payroll window | Data of 10 to 100 data subjects exposed | Contract penalty; formal complaint to the IPDCP |
| 4 Major | 15 to 40 M | Lagune Pay unavailable 4 to 24 hours inside the payroll window (25th to 5th); loss of one large customer | Data of 100 to 1,000 data subjects exposed or altered | IPDCP investigation; loss of a tender |
| 5 Severe | Over 40 M | Lagune Pay unavailable over 24 hours inside the payroll window; salaries paid to wrong accounts | Data of over 1,000 data subjects exposed or altered | IPDCP sanction; criminal proceedings |

## 4. Risk level

Risk score = likelihood x impact (1 to 25).

| Score | Level |
|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | Low |
| 5 to 9 | Medium |
| 10 to 16 | High |
| 20 to 25 | Critical |

Matrix (rows: likelihood, columns: impact):

| L \ I | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| 4 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
| 3 | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |

## 5. Risk acceptance criteria

| Level | Decision rule | Who can accept the residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Low | May be retained without further action | Risk owner |
| Medium | Treat, or retain with a written justification | Risk owner, recorded by the ISMS manager |
| High | Must be treated; the residual risk after treatment must be Medium or lower, unless management decides otherwise in writing | CEO |
| Critical | Must be treated as a priority; cannot be retained at this level | Not acceptable |

## 6. Risk owners

The risk owner is the person with the authority and budget to decide on the treatment. Default owners at Lagune Payroll:

| Area | Risk owner |
|---|---|
| Lagune Pay application and code | Head of Development |
| Production infrastructure, backups, internal tools | Head of Infrastructure |
| Customer support processes | Head of Customer Support |
| HR processes | HR Manager |
| Legal and data protection | CEO |

## 7. Treatment options

For each risk above Low, the risk owner chooses one or more options, in line with ISO/IEC 27005: modify the risk (add or change controls), avoid it (stop the activity), share it (insurance, contract), or retain it. Controls are selected from Annex A or elsewhere, then compared with Annex A to check that no necessary control has been left out (clause 6.1.3 b and c). The result feeds the Statement of Applicability and the risk treatment plan.

## 8. Frequency

The assessment is reviewed at least once a year, and also after a significant change (new product, new hosting provider, new law), after a major incident, or when an audit finding requires it (clause 8.2).
